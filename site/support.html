From the First Cone

Support

From the First Cone plans a youth practice and runs it with you on the field, one drill card at a time. It has 120 drills for soccer, basketball, baseball and softball, and agility.

Questions, bugs and suggestions go to hello@crossedkeys.co. One person reads that address, and it is me.

Planning a practice

  1. Open First Cone. That is the app’s name on the home screen.
  2. Choose a sport. The app opens today’s practice: its drills and the minutes for each.
  3. Tap the day to see another day’s practice. Tap Focus to work on a different skill; any focus runs on any day.
  4. Tap Start practice.

Soccer, basketball and baseball practices run about 56 minutes and start with an agility warm-up. Agility practices run about 30 minutes. The minutes are suggestions.

Changing a practice

Tap Customize practice. Tap a drill to swap it for another, or swipe left to remove it. Agility drills can be added; they go to the end of the practice. The warm-up can be swapped or removed like any other drill.

The app keeps your changes for that sport and focus. Restore default practice puts the original drills back.

Running a practice

  • Each card has the setup, one coaching cue and a way to make the drill harder. The cue and the way to make it harder always show; tap Setup, Why or Explainer to open it.
  • Swipe to move between drills. When a drill’s time is up, Next drill takes you to the next one.
  • Tap play to start the timer and +1 min to add a minute. The app never moves on to the next drill by itself.
  • If you swipe away while a timer runs, the timer pauses. A red button, such as Drill 3 paused, takes you back to it.
  • Tap the tally to count, and Undo to take one back.
  • The screen stays on until you end the practice.
  • Tap End, then End practice, to finish. Timers and tallies are not saved.

When time is up

On iOS 26 and later, the app asks whether it may set alarms the first time you start a practice. If you allow it, the end of each drill rings even with the phone locked.

Before iOS 26, or if you said no, the app plays a bell and vibrates when time is up, while it is open on screen. Nothing rings with the phone locked.

If the alarm stops ringing with the phone locked, check that alarms are allowed for First Cone in the Settings app.

Privacy

The app collects no data, and what it keeps stays on your phone and in your phone’s backups. The privacy policy has the detail.

Requirements

iOS 18 or later, on iPhone. Alarms with the phone locked need iOS 26 or later.
